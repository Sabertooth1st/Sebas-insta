# 14-day plan

Two weeks from the first post to the guide being on sale.

This plan assumes what is already true: the angle is the 177 kg transformation,
the product is **From 177**, the images are made and sitting in `assets/`, and the
bio link points at the landing page in this repo with a mailing list on it.

The goal for the fortnight is **a list of people who want the guide**, and then
the guide going on sale to them. Not followers. Followers are how you get the
list; the list is how you get the sale.

## Before day 1

Four things, an hour in total:

1. Switch on GitHub Pages and put the URL in the Instagram bio. (README →
   *Putting the page online*.)
2. Switch on the email form, or the whole fortnight collects nothing. (README →
   *Turning on the email form*.)
3. Set the bio to one line that says the thing: *Started at 177kg. Link below.*
4. Write the guide. It is twelve pages — it does not need to exist before day 1,
   but it has to exist before day 10, and the days get busier, not quieter.

## The shape of it

- **Days 1-4** — the story. The before and after does the work.
- **Days 5-9** — the method. One rule at a time, which is also proof the guide
  has something in it.
- **Days 10-14** — the launch. Sell to people who already trust you.

## Days 1-4: the story

You already have the single best post you will make for months. Lead with it.

**Day 1 — `assets/before-after.jpg`.** The whole story in one frame. Caption: the
number, the starting point, and where the rest is. Do not explain the method
here. The picture is the hook and the method is the reason to follow.

**Day 2 — `assets/progress-three-stage.jpg`.** The middle frame is the point. A
two-panel post looks like magic; a three-panel one looks like something a person
could do. Caption it with how it felt in the middle, because that is the part
nobody posts.

**Day 3 — the start.** "I started at home, where nobody could see me." Say why.
This is the post that makes people who are too embarrassed to walk into a gym
follow you, and those are the people who buy the guide.

**Day 4 — the worst of it.** The hardest week, the thing you nearly quit over,
the meal you missed most. Specific, not brave.

Post `assets/before-after-story.jpg` to your story on day 1 and again on day 4,
with a link sticker to the page. Stories are where the link actually gets tapped.

Watch **watch time and saves**, not likes. Watch time decides whether the post
keeps getting shown; saves tell you a post was worth keeping.

## Days 5-9: the method

Five days, five rules. They are already written on the landing page, and they are
the spine of the guide:

| Day | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Six days a week. Weightlifting and cardio. |
| 6 | 16:8 — everything inside an eight-hour window. |
| 7 | No carbs, no sugar. For a year. |
| 8 | Protein over carbs. That was the switch. |
| 9 | Starting at home, where nobody could see me. |

One rule per post. Say what it actually meant day to day — what time you ate,
what you ate, what you did when you were out with people. The detail is the
proof. Someone who watches all five should think *this person kept notes*,
because that is exactly what they are being asked to buy.

End days 7 and 9 by asking what people are stuck on. Read every reply. Their
words are the sales copy for days 10-14, and if three people ask the same
question, that question is the first page of the guide.

**Mention the list on days 5, 7 and 9.** Not a pitch — "the full thing is coming,
link in bio if you want it first." This is the whole purpose of the first nine
days. A follower who never joins the list will probably never see the launch.

## Days 10-14: the launch

**Day 10 — it exists.** Publish on Gumroad. Put the link in the card and delete
`data-pending` (README → *Arming a thing for sale*). Email the list first, a few
hours before you post. They waited; they get it first, and the first sales come
from them.

**Day 11 — what is in it.** Photograph the actual pages. People buy a thing they
can see. A twelve-page PDF looks like more than a twelve-page PDF when they can
see the layout.

**Day 12 — one chapter, free.** Give away the single most useful page. The
people who were going to buy will buy faster, not less.

**Day 13 — the price goes up on day 15.** $19 now, $29 after. Say it plainly and
only once. The deadline is real, so honour it — if you extend it, no deadline you
set afterwards will ever work again.

**Day 14 — last call, and the before and after again.** Re-post day 1's image.
Most of the people following you now never saw it, and it is still the best
argument you have.

Then: **raise the price on day 15.** You said you would.

### Two things that lose sales

- **Posting about it once.** Most of your followers will not see any given post.
  Five launch posts is not spam; it is the only way the news arrives.
- **A card that is still `data-pending` on day 10.** Check the live page on your
  phone the moment you publish, not the file on your laptop.

## The presets

`Sebastian Signature` is the second product, and it stays second. Do not launch
it in the same fortnight — two things for sale at once means people choose
instead of buying, and the presets have nothing to do with the story that is
pulling people in. Arm that card once the guide's launch is done, and sell it to
the list separately.

## Hooks that work

The first line decides whether anything else gets read:

- "I spent [time] doing X so you don't have to."
- "Nobody talks about [thing], and it is why [problem] keeps happening."
- "If you're [doing the common thing], stop."
- "[Result] in [timeframe]. Here's exactly how."
- "The mistake that cost me [specific thing]."

Your best one is a number you already own: **177**. Use it. A real number beats a
vague claim every time — and only ever use numbers you can stand behind. The
landing page deliberately claims no current weight, no duration and no total
lost, because those have not been stated. Keep the posts to the same standard.
One unverifiable number is all it takes to make the true ones look invented.

## What to write down

A row per post: date, format, hook, views, watch time, saves, follows gained,
**emails gained**. Five minutes a week.

That last column is the one that matters for these two weeks. A post with 10k
views and no signups lost, and a post with 400 views and 20 signups won.

## What this plan does not do

It will not build an audience from zero in fourteen days. If the account is new,
the realistic outcome is a working format, a list in the low dozens, and a
product ready to sell — and the sales come in month two and three, from posts
you have not made yet.

What it does do is make sure that when the audience does arrive, everything is
already built and pointing the right way. That is the part most people get
wrong, and it is already done here.

If the account already has engaged followers, days 10-14 are where the money
actually happens, and it can happen fast.
