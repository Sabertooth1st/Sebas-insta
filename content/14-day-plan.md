# 14-day plan

Ten days from the first post to the guide being on sale, and five more selling it.

This plan assumes what is already true: the angle is the 177 kg transformation,
the product is **From 177**, the images are made and sitting in `assets/`, and the
landing page with its mailing list is built and waiting to be switched on.

The goal for the fortnight is **a list of people who want the guide**, and then
the guide going on sale to them. Not followers. Followers are how you get the
list; the list is how you get the sale.

## Before day 1

Five things, about an hour:

1. Switch on GitHub Pages and put the URL in the Instagram bio. (README →
   *Putting the page online*.)
2. Switch on the email form — and **pick a provider that can send email, not
   just collect it**. Formspree collects; Buttondown and MailerLite send. Day 10
   needs sending. (README → *Turning on the email form*.)
3. **Send yourself one real address through the form and confirm it arrives.**
   A wrong endpoint fails quietly and the page still looks fine, so without this
   check the fortnight can collect nothing and you find out on day 10.
4. **Open the Gumroad account.** It needs a real name, email and bank details,
   and identity and payout verification are not instant — this is a day-10
   blocker if you leave it to day 10. Publish a $0 test product and buy it
   yourself to prove the path works.
5. Set the Instagram bio to one line that says the thing: *Started at 177kg.
   Link below.*

**And not before day 1, but before day 10: write the guide.** Aim for about
twelve pages. Days 5-9 below fold the writing into the posting, which is the
only reason it fits.

## The shape of it

- **Days 1-4** — the story. The before and after does the work.
- **Days 5-9** — the method. One rule at a time, which is also proof the guide
  has something in it.
- **Days 10-14** — the launch. Sell to people who already trust you.

## Days 1-4: the story

You already have the single best post you will make for months. Lead with it.

**Day 1 — `assets/before-after.jpg`.** The whole story in one frame. Caption: the
number, the starting point, and where the rest is. Do not explain the method
here. The picture is the hook and the method is the reason to follow.

**Day 2 — `assets/progress-three-stage.jpg`.** The middle frame is the point. A
two-panel post looks like magic; a three-panel one looks like something a person
could do. Caption it with how it felt in the middle, because that is the part
nobody posts.

**Day 3 — the start.** "I started at home, where nobody could see me." Say why.
This is the post that makes people who are too embarrassed to walk into a gym
follow you, and those are the people who buy the guide.

**Day 4 — the worst of it.** The hardest week, the thing you nearly quit over,
the meal you missed most. Specific, not brave.

**Put a story up most days, with a link sticker to the page.** Stories are where
the link actually gets tapped, so this is where the signups come from — thirty
seconds a day, and probably the highest-return habit in the fortnight. Post
`assets/before-after-story.jpg` on day 1, on days 5, 7 and 9 when you mention
the list, and every day of the launch.

Watch **watch time and saves**, not likes. Watch time decides whether the post
keeps getting shown; saves tell you a post was worth keeping.

## Days 5-9: the method

Five days, five rules. They are already written on the landing page, and they are
the spine of the guide:

| Day | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Six days a week. Weightlifting and cardio. |
| 6 | 16:8 — everything inside an eight-hour window. |
| 7 | No sugar, no bread, no rice. For a year. |
| 8 | Protein over carbs. That was the switch. |
| 9 | Starting at home — day 3 again, now as method: training with no equipment and no audience. |

**Write the guide as you go.** Each of these five days: write the post, then
expand it into its guide page in the same sitting. Five rules, five pages, and
by the end of day 9 the spine is written. **End of day 7: a complete rough
draft, however ugly. Days 8-9: lay it out and export.** The guide is the only
part of this fortnight with no slack in it, and this is where it gets written.

One rule per post. Say what it actually meant day to day — what time you ate,
what you ate, what you did when you were out with people. The detail is the
proof. Someone who watches all five should think *this person kept notes*,
because that is exactly what they are being asked to buy.

End days 7 and 9 by asking what people are stuck on. Read every reply. Their
words are the sales copy for days 10-14, and if three people ask the same
question, that question is the first page of the guide.

**Mention the list on days 5, 7 and 9.** Not a pitch — "the full thing is coming,
link in bio if you want it first." This is the whole purpose of the first nine
days. A follower who never joins the list will probably never see the launch.

## Days 10-14: the launch

**Day 10 — it exists.** Publish on Gumroad. Put the link in the card and delete
`data-pending` (README → *Arming a thing for sale*). Email the list first, a few
hours before you post. They waited; they get it first, and the first sales come
from them. Write that email on day 9 — day 10 is the busiest day of the
fortnight and an improvised launch email reads like one.

**The welcome email matters more than it looks.** Someone who joins on day 2
otherwise gets a form confirmation, eight days of silence, and then a sales
email from a stranger. Set up an automatic first email the moment the list
exists: the five rules on one page, or the printable summary. It proves the
guide will be real, and it means day 10 lands on someone who has already had
something useful from you.

**Day 11 — what is in it.** Photograph the actual pages. People buy a thing they
can see, and a PDF looks like more than a PDF once they can see the layout.

**Day 12 — one chapter, free.** Give away the single most useful page. The
people who were going to buy will buy faster, not less.

**Day 13 — the price goes up on day 17.** $19 now, $29 after — one week from
launch, which is exactly what the card promises. Say it plainly on day 13 and
once more on day 14, then leave it. The deadline is real, so honour it; if you
extend it, no deadline you set afterwards will ever work again. Be realistic
about its size, too: against a list in the low dozens this moves two or three
sales forward. It is worth doing because it builds the habit of setting a
deadline and keeping it, which is what makes the next launch work — it is not
where the money is.

**Day 14 — last call, and the before and after again.** Re-post day 1's image.
Most of the people following you now never saw it, and it is still the best
argument you have.

Then: **raise the price on day 17.** You said you would.

That is a manual edit in two places — the card in `index.html` and the list in
`product/README.md`. Nothing does it for you, and until you do, the card
undercharges against Gumroad.

**If you miss a day, do not double up.** Post the missed one next and shift
everything back. The order matters more than the dates, and trying to catch up
is how people quit in week two.

**Keep every reply in one note.** The questions people send on days 7 and 9 are
the guide's FAQ page and the day-11 caption, in their own words. That note is
worth more than any hook template.

### Two things that lose sales

- **Posting about it once.** Most of your followers will not see any given post.
  Five launch posts is not spam; it is the only way the news arrives.
- **A card that is still `data-pending` on day 10.** Check the live page on your
  phone the moment you publish, not the file on your laptop.

## The presets

`Sebastian Signature` is the second product, and it stays second. Do not launch
it in the same fortnight — two things for sale at once means people choose
instead of buying, and the presets have nothing to do with the story that is
pulling people in. Arm that card once the guide's launch is done, and sell it to
the list separately.

## Hooks that work

**Read this before the list, not after it.** Only ever use numbers you can stand
behind, and never tell a stranger what to eat. Two patterns that circulate
everywhere are exactly the ones you must not write — "[Result] in [timeframe]"
is a blank for a total and a duration you have never stated, and "If you're
[doing the thing], stop" turns your own diet into an instruction to someone
whose health you know nothing about. They are deliberately not on this list.

The first line decides whether anything else gets read:

- "Nobody talks about [thing], and it is why [problem] keeps happening."
- "The mistake that cost me [specific thing]."
- "I was [specific situation]. Here is what I changed."
- "Nobody tells you [honest detail] about [thing you did]."
- "This is what [a number you have actually stated] looked like."

Your best asset is a number you already own: **177**. Use it — a real number
beats a vague claim every time. The landing page deliberately claims no current
weight and no total lost, because neither has been stated. Keep the posts to the
same standard: one unverifiable number is all it takes to make the true ones
look invented.

## What to write down

A row per post: date, format, hook, views, watch time, saves, follows gained,
**emails gained**. Five minutes a week.

That last column is the one that matters for these two weeks. A post with 10k
views and no signups lost, and a post with 400 views and 20 signups won.

## What this plan does not do

It will not build an audience from zero in fourteen days. If the account is new,
the realistic outcome is a working format, a list in the low dozens, and a
product ready to sell — and the sales come in month two and three, from posts
you have not made yet.

What it does do is make sure that when the audience does arrive, the page is
built and pointing the right way. That part is done; the five switches in the
*Before day 1* list still need flipping.

If the account already has engaged followers, days 10-14 are where the money
actually happens, and it can happen fast.
